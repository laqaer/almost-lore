# House style

**Voice:** a sharp archivist who has seen every myth and still enjoys the good ones. Wry, exact,
warm. Never smug toward players who get it wrong: the joke is on the myth, not the reader.

- Short declarative sentences. One vivid, checkable detail beats three adjectives.
- Numbers are exact where the record is exact and hedged where it isn't ("about 1,500",
  "compilers disagree between the third and fourth"). Never average two conflicting figures into a
  fake precise one.
- Say what the record shows, then what it doesn't. Uncertainty stays visible.
- No invented dialogue, no invented interiority ("he must have felt…").
- British/American spelling: American. Dates: 15 January 1919 in prose; 1919 in labels.
- Verdict words are always capitalised in UI copy: HAPPENED, ALMOST, LORE.
- Microcopy is part of the brand: rank titles, empty states, error messages and buttons all get the
  same care as headlines. ("No peeking. No googling. No Uncle Dave.")
- Never write "Wordle-style", "like Kahoot", "Balderdash-style". Describe the mechanic itself.
- Solemn subjects (deaths, atrocities, disasters) get a straight face: no puns, no ink-splatter,
  no joke rank titles.

## Case Files (longform)
- 1,400–2,400 words, 5–8 sections with plain headings, opening with the hook as travellers repeat it
  and then the record.
- End with "Why the hook is not the essay" or an equivalent turn: what the short version misses.
- `sourcesNote` names the kinds of sources used and what is disputed. Original prose only; never
  paraphrase a single source's structure.
